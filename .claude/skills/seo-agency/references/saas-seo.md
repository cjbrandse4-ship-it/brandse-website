# SaaS / B2B software

## Where buyers research in 2026
- About half of B2B software buyers now start research in an AI chatbot (G2 buyer survey, April 2026).
- When ChatGPT recommends software it mostly cites third-party pages rather than the vendor's own site (one 40-category study: ≈87% third-party). Only a small share of Google top-3 pages for B2B queries are also cited by ChatGPT/Claude.
- Review sites are a baseline, not a guarantee: studies disagree (from ~0% to ~8% of citations). Be listed with real reviews, then focus on roundups, comparisons and community presence.

## Page inventory, in priority order
1. **Comparison pages** — "[Product] vs [Competitor]" for every competitor buyers actually consider.
2. **Alternative pages** — "[Competitor] alternatives" and "[Competitor] alternative for [segment]".
3. **Pricing page** — plans, limits, what's included, FAQs, in plain HTML (AI cites visible pricing).
4. **Use-case / segment pages** — "[category] software for [industry/size/region/language]".
5. **Feature pages** — one per major job-to-be-done, with screenshots and specifics.
6. **Integration pages** — what syncs, how to set it up, limits.
7. **Case studies** — named customer, situation, numbers, quote (written permission).
8. **Educational content** buyers search before buying (templates, calculators, how-tos), each linked to the matching feature.

Early-stage products: most new pages should be bottom-of-funnel (1–6) until every real competitor and segment has a page.

## Comparison page anatomy
- Opening paragraph: who each tool is best for, in two or three sentences AI can quote.
- Comparison table: pricing (with date), key features, limits, support, languages, integrations.
- "Choose [competitor] if… / Choose [product] if…" — honest; one-sided pages lose trust with buyers and AI.
- Migration section: how to switch, what transfers.
- FAQ, last updated date, sources for competitor facts.
- `SoftwareApplication` schema for your product only.

## Proof assets that get cited
- **Original research** from aggregated, anonymized product data ("State of [industry] [year]"): benchmarks, seasonality, averages. Only aggregates across many customers, with the right under the terms (see `compliance.md`).
- **Case studies** with real metrics.
- **Founder/practitioner credibility**: if the team works in the industry, say so with specifics.

## Off-site program
- Review-site listings and a steady review request to happy customers (same neutral ask to all).
- Roundup/listicle outreach for "best [category]" and "[competitor] alternatives".
- Integration marketplaces.
- Industry associations, events, podcasts, newsletters.
- Genuine founder participation in relevant communities.
- YouTube walkthroughs and how-tos.

## Niche and language advantages
Underserved segments (a specific trade, region or language) are the fastest wins: fewer competitors, clearer intent, and AI engines short on good sources. Build complete, native-language page sets for them.
